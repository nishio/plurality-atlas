What is the Plurality movement in Japan?

In Japan, the book *Plurality* spread from around 2023 as volunteers read it, translated it and held events about it. A Japanese edition came out in May 2025, reading groups and reviews followed across the country, and the book's name even appeared in a political party's policy platform.

When a book written abroad reaches Japan, the usual path is that a publisher buys the translation rights, a translator translates it, and it goes on sale in bookshops. Plurality took a slightly different path. Because the book was published from the start in a form anyone could use freely, readers in Japan began by translating it themselves, reading it together and holding events. This article follows that story in order.

In short

The book Plurality by Audrey Tang and E. Glen Weyl spread in Japan from around 2023, as volunteer communities read it, translated it and introduced it to others. In May 2025 a Japanese edition, PLURALITY: 対立を創造に変える、協働テクノロジーと民主主義の未来 (roughly, "Turning conflict into creation: the future of collaborative technology and democracy"), was published, followed by reading groups and reviews [1]. The book also appears in the manifesto (the policy platform a party puts forward in elections) of the political party Team Mirai [2].

What Plurality itself means is explained in a separate article. This one is about how the book has been received in Japan.

For example

Plurality Tokyo (April 12, 2023). This event was held in Tokyo during the week of "ETH Tokyo", a gathering about cryptographic technology. Tang gave the keynote, and Hirokazu Nishio produced a full Japanese translation of it. Kevin Owocki of Gitcoin and Joi Ito were among the other speakers. Haruyuki Seki of Code for Japan gave the opening remarks, and Rikuto Niimi, then 19 years old, led the organizing [3][4].

A volunteer Japanese translation. The collaborative translation led by Nishio publishes its text and figures under CC0 (a waiver of copyright, so anyone may use them freely) [5]. As of July 2024, 44 people in the Japanese-speaking community were working together on a public wiki, each at whatever time suited them [6].

The Japanese edition and the authors' visit (May 2025). On May 2, 2025, Cybozu Shiki Books (distributed by ライツ社, Raitsusha) published the Japanese edition. It was translated by Hiroo Yamagata, with a commentary by Ken Suzuki [1]. A series of events called "Tokyo Plurality Week 2025" coincided with the two authors' visit to Japan. They included a conversation between Tang and Yoichi Ochiai at Yasuda Auditorium of the University of Tokyo, and a conference to mark the publication at Keio University's Mita campus [7].

In more detail

Before the edition: reading a book anyone can use, in Japanese (2023–2024)

The original was published on GitHub (a website for making programs and documents together) under CC0 while it was still being written. As early as March 2023, yomoyomo of the blog "YAMDAS現更新履歴" introduced the book with a provisional Japanese title and noted that its license was CC0 [8].

At an event during Weyl's visit to Japan on January 3, 2024, Nishio described reading the book as a book about technology for creating a society brimming with teamwork, which is the mission of Cybozu, the company Nishio works for. Nishio said the term "broad listening" in particular was taken to mean strengthening the ability to understand the opinions of many people [9]. → Broad listening

The translation was framed as a project whose act of translating would itself create a place for people to talk things over together, raising the general level of understanding in Japan [10]. In March 2024, in a conversation with Tang, Haruyuki Seki told her that the book was being translated into Japanese and that a conference including Plurality Tokyo was planned for July [11].

Summer 2024: a visit and an announcement

In July 2024, Tang and Weyl came to Japan for "Funding the Commons Tokyo 2024", held at the United Nations University, and Nishio gave a presentation titled "⿻Plurality in Japan" [6]. During that event, Cybozu Shiki Books announced that it was producing a Japanese edition and opened pre-orders [12].

In a piece written for the Japanese edition, Weyl looks back on the day of a conversation held at the National Museum of Emerging Science and Innovation (Miraikan) on July 26, 2024, as the most meaningful day of his professional life [13]. In his commentary, Ken Suzuki writes that this was when he first met the two authors, and that he found common ground with his own book なめらかな社会とその敵 (The Nameraka Society and Its Enemies) [14]. → The Nameraka Society

The book and an election overlap

In the same summer of 2024, Takahiro Anno put broad listening into practice in the election for Governor of Tokyo. This case is written into chapter 5-4, "Augmented Deliberation", in both the English and Japanese editions. The same chapter in the Traditional Chinese (Taiwan) edition does not contain this paragraph [15][14].

Ken Suzuki's commentary describes Anno's run as one of the new developments in the Plurality movement drawing strong attention worldwide. He also writes that in 2025 he and Anno started "Digital Democracy 2030" [14]. → Takahiro Anno's 2024 Tokyo campaign · Digital Democracy 2030

The Japanese edition (May 2, 2025)

The Japanese edition is an A5-size book of 624 pages, priced at 3,000 yen before tax. It includes a special foreword for the Japanese edition by the two authors [1]. The publisher described Plurality as technology for cooperating across social differences, and presented it as an idea paired with, and set against, the "singularity" [1].

In the Japanese edition, chapter 3-0 is titled "⿻プルラリティ（多元性）とは？" ("What is ⿻ Plurality (multiplicity)?") [16]. The Traditional Chinese edition in Taiwan layers the words "數位" (digital) and "多元宇宙" (multiverse) onto the same symbol ⿻ [17]. → Shuwei and ⿻

Reading together spreads (2025–2026)

After publication, reviews were written by Shigeki Uno, Yuko Tanaka, Keigo Komamura, Kan Suzuki and others. In her review in that series, Yuko Tanaka wrote that Sunji Lee's short paperback テクノ専制とコモンへの道 (roughly, "Techno-despotism and the road to the commons", 2025) points in the same direction as the book [18].

Reading groups met in many places.

  • Haruka Shimazu learned about the book when she heard Tang and Anno speak at the Code for Japan Summit in November 2024. When the Japanese edition came out, Shimazu held a reading group with fellow local assembly members and has kept a record of each chapter [19].
  • In Dazaifu, Fukuoka Prefecture, a reading group was held in which the mayor also took part (July 2025) [20].
  • Masato Tahara started a reading group in February 2025, before the Japanese edition was out, reading with the help of ChatGPT [21].

In December 2025 the book was nominated for the "Business Book Grand Prix Chosen by Readers" [22].

Entering the language of politics

The political party Team Mirai, which Anno launched in May 2025, added a supplementary note about Plurality to the "Digital Democracy" chapter of its manifesto and described Plurality as an important guideline for advancing digital democracy [2]. Japanese Wikipedia also summarizes that Team Mirai takes Plurality, together with digital democracy, as a guideline, and that it sets out five values, such as not stoking division and not jumping to conclusions about anything [23]. → Team Mirai

In October 2025, one user posted on X (formerly Twitter) that people the user recognized as Team Mirai supporters had begun adding the "⿻" symbol after their names [24].

What is debated

  • Worry about dogmatism (turning ideas into rules no one questions). Kazuki Ichida judged the book so broad and comprehensive that it is a book to be used like an encyclopedia. Ichida also questioned the way it treats Japan's quality control (QC) activities as an almost unqualified success. Ichida then expressed concern that people in Japan who sympathize with the book might become dogmatic, giving one of the proposals to Team Mirai's policy repository as an example [25].
  • Criticism that it may become just a slogan. A note.com writer going by 一般人 ("an ordinary person") argued that with the Plurality Team Mirai advocates, it is unclear who decides and who is responsible, and that the ideals run ahead while the design of institutions is left empty. The writer also criticizes that the citizen-participation tools cannot be said to be working in practice, and that rather than overcoming division, they make division harder to see [26].
  • A harsh reading note on an introductory book. One reader gave Sunji Lee's paperback a low rating. The reader asks whether Taiwan's systems work well because trust in democracy was there to begin with, and writes that the conditions for Plurality to work look like the very society Plurality aims for, so the argument feels circular [27].
  • Can it really be implemented? One piece reads a March 2026 conversation between Tang and Hiroki Azuma as a contrast between a person who writes specifications and a person who keeps asking questions, and asks whether Plurality can actually be put into practice [28].
  • A common misunderstanding: the Japanese edition itself is not CC0. What is explicitly marked CC0 is the translation and figures in the volunteer translation repository [5]. Ken Suzuki's commentary was released separately under CC0 [14]. The license of the printed book as a whole could not be confirmed in the materials this site has collected.

Where it fits in Plurality

The book itself takes up Japan in several places. Chapter 3-3, "The Lost Dao", has a section called "Quality Control and Nameraka Society", covering Deming's quality control and Ken Suzuki's Nameraka Society [29]. Chapter 5-4, "Augmented Deliberation", covers Anno's run for governor [15].

In Japan, Plurality has been talked about especially in connection with the practice of digital democracy and broad listening [2][14]. Articles to read alongside this one:
