Who is Hirokazu Nishio?

A researcher at Cybozu Labs in Japan, who helped edit the English edition of the book *Plurality* and led the project that published its Japanese edition. Nishio also gave technical support to Takahiro Anno's campaign for Governor of Tokyo, and works on developing Kouchou AI, which uses AI to organize the voices of many people.

Trace how the Japanese edition of PLURALITY came about, and you arrive at this name. The same person is also on the ground in the work that Japan calls "broad listening".

Note that this article is about the operator of this site. For the topics Nishio is involved in and how they are handled here, see "About this site".

In short

Hirokazu Nishio (西尾泰和, NISHIO Hirokazu) is a researcher at Cybozu Labs [1]. Nishio was one of the people who helped edit the English edition of Plurality, and led the project to publish the Japanese edition [2][3]. In Japan, Nishio also takes part in the practice and development of broad listening (technology that uses AI to organize the voices of many people and deliver them) [4][5].

According to the profile on the website of the Mitou Foundation, Nishio earned a doctorate (science) at the Nara Institute of Science and Technology, and has worked at Cybozu Labs since 2007 [6]. In fiscal 2002 Nishio was recognized as a "Super Creator" in IPA's Mitou Youth program, and later joined in launching "Mitou Junior", a program for young people [7][1]. → Mitou

Connection to Plurality

Editing the English edition. In the book's list of contributors (⿻ credits), Nishio's name appears under "Editing" [2].

How the Japanese edition came about. Nishio told Cybozu Shiki (Cybozu's web magazine) the story as follows. Nishio first learned of the idea at the 2023 event "PLURALITY TOKYO 2023". Learning that the English edition was being written on GitHub (an online space for working together), Nishio started making a Japanese machine translation. Word of a talk Nishio gave at an in-house presentation reached Cybozu Shiki Books, and this led to publication [3]. The translator of the Japanese edition is Hiroo Yamagata [8], and the manuscript of the Japanese translation is released under CC0 (no rights reserved) [9].

A gathering of readers. In a July 2024 talk, Nishio reported that 44 people were gathering in a public Japanese-language wiki to build a network of knowledge [10].

Work in Japan

Takahiro Anno's campaign for Governor of Tokyo (2024). In the book Broad Listening, Nishio writes of taking part as one of Takahiro Anno's technical supporters. The two were fellow mentors at Mitou Junior, and in May 2024 Anno got in touch wanting to hear about Plurality. Nishio records introducing Anno to Haruyuki Seki, and that at a meeting of the three, Seki introduced Talk to the City [4]. → Takahiro Anno's 2024 Tokyo campaign

Developing Kouchou AI. For Digital Democracy 2030's Kouchou AI, Nishio is recorded as one of the main developers, with 354 of the 2,756 commits (records of changes) made from February 2025 to February 2026 [5].

The Broad Listening book. Nishio is one of the authors of the book on broad listening that its practitioners wrote together [11].

Team Mirai (2025–). Nishio volunteers on the technical side of Team Mirai's projects, without being employed by the party. Nishio explained the "talking manifesto" used in the 2025 House of Councillors election in section 6.1 of the Broad Listening book (written by Nishio) and on Nishio's blog. There Nishio writes of being not a member of Team Mirai's engineering team but a "long-time supporter" since the Tokyo governor's race, not in a position to take part in the party's decisions, and of building a system to collect and classify the proposals submitted on GitHub [15][16]. In a September 2025 FAQ, Nishio answered that there was no intention of being employed, but that volunteering on themes of interest was possible [17].

Main ideas

Broad listening. Nishio explains it as the reverse of broadcasting, which copies one person's message and delivers it to many. Broad listening, in this explanation, is technology that organizes the many voices gathered, puts them into a form a person can take in, and delivers them [12]. → Broad listening

The river of democracy. In a November 2025 talk, Nishio used the image of digital technology flowing into the river of democracy. Between democracy as it has been and an "AI philosopher-king" system, in which AI governs like a king, there may be something better; the digital democracy movement, Nishio said, is the search for it. The talk traces the flow from Polis (2012 onward) to Kouchou AI [13]. → Digital democracy

Words as public goods. At a January 2024 event in Tokyo, Nishio gave a presentation treating words and translations as shared property that belongs to everyone (public goods) [14].

What is debated

There have been the following points about projects Nishio worked on as a builder.

Looking back on the talking manifesto (as an insider). Nishio's own review lists as problems that people who made proposals did not get a sense that they had been heard; that revision proposals to the same passage collided, so the policy staff had to read them all and work out what each wanted; and that people unused to GitHub used it as a discussion forum, causing confusion that had not been anticipated [15][16].

Criticism from outside. One note article by a member of the public reports that "Idobata", one of the parts of the talking manifesto, has been criticised because "the quality of the original proposals is low" and "the review process needs to be improved", and argues that this kind of open-source-style civic participation leaves it unclear who is responsible for quality control and accountability [18]. Another note article writes that the Idobata Vision is hard for first-time users to understand and feels like a public-comment procedure [19].

Is it still in use? In February 2026, Shinta Nakayama, the main author of the Broad Listening book, wrote that Team Mirai no longer uses Talk to the City or its successor, Kouchou AI [20].

Criticism of Team Mirai itself is collected in the article on Team Mirai.
