Timeline of Plurality and digital democracy in Japan

In 2023, an event championing Plurality, "Plurality Tokyo", was held in Tokyo. After the 2024 Tokyo governor's election, activity spread to the Tokyo Metropolitan Government's long-term strategy, an open-source tool called Kouchou AI, a Japanese edition of the book, and a new party entering parliament. This timeline follows those events in date order.

You may see phrases like "Tokyo analyzes residents' opinions with AI" or "digital democracy" in the news, but it is hard to tell when and where this started. In Japan, over the past few years, the same tools and ideas have spread step by step: from a small event, to an election, to the Tokyo Metropolitan Government, to cities and towns, and to the national parliament. This article lays out those events in date order.

How the book has been read and translated is covered in detail in the article on the Plurality movement in Japan. Use this one as an overall "map" that also includes elections, government and tools.

In short

In 2023, "Plurality Tokyo", an event championing the ideas of the book Plurality by Audrey Tang and E. Glen Weyl, was held in Tokyo [1]. In the summer of 2024, in the election for Governor of Tokyo, Takahiro Anno used broad listening (a way of hearing many voices with the help of AI), an idea that appears in the book, in his campaign [4].

From that point, activity spread to the drafting of the Tokyo Metropolitan Government's long-term strategy [17], an open-source tool (one whose inner workings anyone can see and use) called Kouchou AI [20], the publication of a Japanese edition in 2025 [22], and a new political party, Team Mirai, winning seats in parliament [28][31].

2023 to early 2024: events and translation communities

Date Event
2023-04-12 Plurality Tokyo is held in Tokyo, with a keynote by Tang [1]. → The Plurality movement in Japan
2024-01-03 At "Glen in Japan", an event during Weyl's visit to Japan, Hirokazu Nishio presents "Words as Public Goods" [2]
2024-05-20 Publication of the English edition is announced. It can be read free on the web, and printed copies can also be ordered [3]. → Plurality
2024-05-23 Haruyuki Seki, Anno and Nishio meet, and Seki introduces Talk to the City (a tool that uses AI to sort opinions and draw them as a chart). On May 30, Nishio tries it on about 20,000 AI-related public comments (public comment is a procedure in which a government presents a draft and widely invites opinions on it) [4]

Late 2024: the governor's election and the Tokyo Metropolitan Government

Date Event
2024-06-06 Takahiro Anno announces at Tokyo City Hall that he will run for governor. A full transcript of the press conference is published the next day [5]
2024-06-20 Anno publishes his manifesto (list of policies) and makes it possible for anyone to propose changes on GitHub (a website where documents can be revised together) [6]. → Takahiro Anno's 2024 Tokyo campaign
2024-07-07 Voting and counting in the governor's election. Anno receives 154,638 votes and finishes fifth [4][7]
2024-07-25 At "Funding the Commons Tokyo 2024" at the United Nations University, Nishio presents "⿻Plurality in Japan" [8]
2024-07-26 A conversation with the two authors is held at the National Museum of Emerging Science and Innovation (Miraikan). Weyl later looks back on this as the most meaningful day of his professional life [9]
2024-09-30 Anno's campaign releases programs it built for the election (three of them, including the AI avatar "AI-Anno") [10]
2024-10-26 Anno's campaign announces that it helped Nippon TV's coverage of the House of Representatives election with broad listening [11]
2024-11-22 The Tokyo Metropolitan Government begins inviting opinions on "Shin Tokyo 2050 (provisional name)" [12]. On the same day, Anno becomes an adviser to GovTech Tokyo (the organization in charge of digitalizing the metropolitan government) [13]. → Broad listening for Tokyo's 2050 strategy
2024-12-03 In a policy speech to the Tokyo Metropolitan Assembly, the governor says that broad listening should be brought into many areas of the metropolitan government, and that Shin Tokyo 2050 is the test case [14]
2024-12-16 The metropolitan government publishes interim results on 11,622 opinions and extends the call until December 20 [15]

2025: DD2030, the Japanese edition and Team Mirai

Date Event
2025-01-16 Anno announces the launch of "Digital Democracy 2030 (DD2030)". It is a project to build open-source tools and let local governments and political parties use them free of charge [16]
2025-01-31 The metropolitan government publishes the results of a broad listening analysis of 27,915 items of data, together with the draft "2050 Tokyo Strategy" [17][18]
2025-03 The 2050 Tokyo Strategy is adopted. The metropolitan government's public-relations publication writes that about 30,000 opinions were analyzed with broad listening and used as a reference in drafting it [19]
2025-03-16 DD2030 releases "Kouchou AI" as open source [20]
2025-04-30 The town of Utazu in Kagawa Prefecture publishes the results of a trial of Kouchou AI. It reports that 396 free-form answers from a residents' survey could be organized in about 10 minutes [21]. → Broad listening in local governments
2025-05-02 The Japanese edition of PLURALITY goes on sale (Cybozu Shiki Books, distributed by ライツ社 [Raitsusha], translated by Hiroo Yamagata, commentary by Ken Suzuki) [22]
2025-05-06 to 13 "Tokyo Plurality Week 2025", timed to the two authors' visit to Japan. Events include a lecture at Yasuda Auditorium of the University of Tokyo, a publication event at Keio University's Mita campus, and a book-launch talk at Roppongi Tsutaya Books [23]
2025-05-07 Anno steps down as a board member of DD2030 for the sake of political neutrality [16]
2025-05-08 At a press conference, Anno announces the founding of a new party, Team Mirai [24]
2025-05-16 Team Mirai publishes a very early version (ver0.1) of its manifesto. It gathers opinions through channels including "Idobata Policy", where people send their views by talking with an AI, and releases ver0.2 on June 10 and ver1.0 on July 5 [25]
2025-06-13 Iwate Prefecture publishes the results of its test of broad listening with Kouchou AI [26]
2025-07-02 Shibuya Ward in Tokyo publishes the results of analyzing 6,037 free-form answers from its residents' opinion survey with Kouchou AI [27]
2025-07-20 In the count for the House of Councillors election (a chamber of Japan's parliament), Team Mirai wins 1 seat (about 2.6% of the vote) and meets the requirements to count as a political party [28]
2025-10-02 Team Mirai launches "Mirai Marumie Seiji Shikin" (みらい まる見え政治資金), which shows how political funds flow [29]
2025-10-16 Team Mirai launches "Mirai Gikai" (みらい議会) [30]

2026: parliament, a party convention and the Broad Listening book

Date Event
2026-02-08 In the 51st House of Representatives election (the other chamber of parliament), 11 Team Mirai candidates are elected (a total of 3,813,749 votes across 8 proportional-representation blocks, 6.66% of the vote) [31]
2026-05-21 In the Internal Affairs and Communications Committee of the House of Councillors, Anno asks questions about a bill amending the law on preventing the misuse of mobile phones. He says the questions were based on voices including 264 interviews (about 47 hours in total) gathered through Mirai Gikai [32]
2026-07-18 Team Mirai's first party convention, "Team Mirai Summit 2026". More than 200 party members gather at the venue, and about 1,100 people, including those online, take part in votes by smartphone [33]
2026-08-02 "Digital Democracy Summit 2026", an event on the DD2030 side. Tang's participation on the day is announced [34]. It is a separate event from the party convention "Team Mirai Summit"
2026-09 (planned) A book on broad listening, Broad Listening (Japanese title 政治・自治体・企業に広がるブロードリスニング, roughly "Broad listening spreading through politics, local government and business"). It was written in the DD2030 community with Shinta Nakayama as lead author, and according to DD2030's page it is due from Impress around September [35][36]

Reading the flow

The book came first, and practice caught up later. From 2023 to early 2024, people who gathered at Plurality Tokyo and elsewhere read the original, which had been published in a form anyone could use freely (CC0) [1][2]. According to section 4-1 of the book Broad Listening, Nishio introduced Anno to Haruyuki Seki, and encountering Talk to the City there was what led to its use in the election [4].

The governor's election was a turning point. Chapter 5-4 of Plurality, "Augmented Deliberation", writes Anno's election into the book as an example of new ways of discussion starting to appear in elections too [37].

Government took it up. According to chapter 5 of the book Broad Listening, the Tokyo Metropolitan Government announced that it had used broad listening to analyze its call for opinions and had revised how the strategy's policies were grouped [38]. However, the same chapter 5 examines this and finds that much of the change came from changes in the times or updates to earlier policies, and that the story that "broad listening changed policy" is at least partly overstated [38].

The tools became "everyone's". Because DD2030 released Kouchou AI as open source, local governments such as the town of Utazu, Iwate Prefecture and Shibuya Ward were able to analyze the free-form answers in their own surveys [20][21][26][27].

A party entered the system. Team Mirai won seats in the 2025 House of Councillors election and the 2026 House of Representatives election, and it has used the voices it gathered in parliamentary questions and held its party convention's votes by smartphone [28][31][32][33]. This article lists these events as facts. It does not judge whether any party or policy is good or bad.
