Who is Ryo Shimizu?

A Japanese programmer and entrepreneur known as shi3z, and a Mitou "Super Creator" of fiscal 2004. At a 2026 academic workshop, Shimizu spoke alongside Hirokazu Nishio, and afterward wrote frankly about how Plurality and broad listening sounded to someone hearing about them from the outside.

How do Plurality and broad listening sound to people who have no particular interest in them? One engineer left an answer in plain words.

In short

Ryo Shimizu (清水亮) is a programmer and entrepreneur known online as shi3z. According to an IPA document, Shimizu founded the company Ubiquitous Entertainment in 2003 [5].

There is a book, 軍都を歩く (Walking the military cities), by an author with the same name. Shimizu has written on note, half-jokingly, that people at the conference might be mixing Shimizu up with that person. They are different people [2].

Connection to Plurality

The sources at hand show no direct contact with the book Plurality or with its authors, Tang and Weyl. The point of contact is a single joint appearance in 2026.

Speaking alongside Nishio at an academic workshop. On September 13, 2026, the 15th annual conference of the Japan Association for Social Informatics was held at the Siebold campus of the University of Nagasaki. At its workshop "生成AI時代のシビックテック・デザイン ― 参加や社会課題解決はAIとどのように共進化するか" (Civic tech design in the age of generative AI: how will participation and solving social problems co-evolve with AI?), chaired by Yuya Shibuya, two people gave talks [1].

  • Ryo Shimizu (in person) — "AIと人間の共生から見た社会課題解決の未来" (The future of solving social problems, seen from the coexistence of AI and humans)
  • Hirokazu Nishio (online) — "Plurality／ブロードリスニング／デジタル民主主義とシビックテック" (Plurality, broad listening, digital democracy and civic tech)

Plurality seen from the outside. In a note post after the workshop, Shimizu wrote openly of being puzzled on seeing the materials in advance: what is digital democracy, and, even harder to understand, what is this word "Plurality"? [3]

After hearing Nishio's talk, Shimizu summed up this understanding. Plurality means recognizing that the opinions of the whole, and dissenting opinions, exist, and building relationships of cooperation on that basis. Broad listening is the opposite of broadcasting: gathering the opinions of many people. With AI, it might be possible to hear even the individual voices that could not be picked up before [3].

Shimizu then wrote frankly that, being neither a politician nor a civil servant, Shimizu did not find it a very interesting theme. In substance: Shimizu is someone who wants to do whatever is most fun, so if that could only be achieved by listening to minority voices it would be interesting, but Shimizu has not yet found such a theme [3].

Because it is a record written by a speaker at the event, it is one example of how the words Plurality and broad listening were received by an engineer with little interest in politics or public administration.

Links with Mitou

In the second half of fiscal 2004, Shimizu was selected for IPA's Mitou software creation program as chief creator of "a workflow-oriented next-generation document application platform" (under PM Katashi Nagao), and was also recognized as a "Super Creator" [4][5].

According to IPA's recognition document, Shimizu was then head of the company Ubiquitous Entertainment, and developed software such as ZEKE, which manages information by dividing it into small units called "articles" and gathers them for retrieval to suit a purpose [5].

Main ideas

The same note post records the ideas Shimizu spoke about at the workshop [3].

Civic tech starting from one's own problems. To increase the number of people who join civic tech (citizens using technology to solve social problems), Shimizu says, it is better to call out that joining civic tech is the quickest way to get rid of your everyday frustrations, rather than using big words about the public good. The ideal, in this view, is that solving your own troubles ends up helping others and leads to a contribution to humanity [3].

Humanity as the subject. Shimizu also argues that when people are asked to help for the sake of a particular city, those with no connection to that city feel less like helping. Making "humanity" the subject brings more people to cooperate [3].

Government should offer a window that AI can use. At the venue, Shimizu said that governments should not only publish data but also build systems, something like an open MCP (a common way of connecting AI to outside tools and data), through which AI can fetch data and file applications. Shimizu also replied that AI can resolve the differences in the formats of open data that have varied from one local government to another, and that the cost of that computation can be left to the users' own machines [3].

Publishing results is the democratization of AI. Shimizu closes by saying that making open source the techniques for running large AI models on one's own computer is, Shimizu came to understand, the democratization of AI in the true sense, and something in the spirit of civic tech [3].
